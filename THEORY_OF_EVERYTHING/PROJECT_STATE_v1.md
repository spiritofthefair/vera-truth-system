## Transformation and evolution
Transformation and evolution are distinct.
- Transformation = one configuration-to-configuration change.
- Evolution = the lineage/composition of transformations through state space and time/causal order.

Current evolution model inside `D`:

`D = (Omega, C, K, E)`

where conceptually:
- `Omega` = possible configurations;
- `C` = constraints;
- `K` = allowed or weighted transitions;
- `E` = composition of transitions into evolution.

The framework must support sequential, concurrent, one-to-many, many-to-one, many-to-many, feedback, reconvergence, and path-dependent evolution without assuming any of these are fundamental ontology.

## Transformation-closure rule
For every candidate physical process, explicitly account for what changed, what was preserved, what transferred, what became inaccessible, what became distributed or correlated, and what genuinely disappeared, if anything.

`LOCAL INFORMATION LOSS != GLOBAL INFORMATION DESTRUCTION`

No degree of freedom may be silently discarded. An apparent terminal state must be classified as one of:
1. mapped output state;
2. inaccessible but conserved/correlated state;
3. explicit boundary condition;
4. genuine information-destroying transition with measurable consequences;
5. `UNKNOWN`.

## Black-hole closure test
Primary bounded experiment:

`pre-collapse state -> gravitational collapse -> horizon / black-hole regime -> Hawking emission -> late radiation -> evaporation / successor state`

The test asks whether one complete evolution can preserve global quantum consistency while reproducing general relativity, Hawking thermality, black-hole entropy, energy accounting, Page-curve behavior, causal consistency, and observed low-energy physics.

The historical failure boundary is the pure-to-mixed information-loss problem in semiclassical black-hole evaporation. Modern Page-curve / island results are treated as strong theoretical evidence in controlled models, not as a complete empirically verified four-dimensional microscopic evaporation theory.
