## Verified input routes

### Route A — Lorentz-invariant massless spin-2 behavior
Weinberg (1964) showed that Lorentz invariance and the pole structure of the S-matrix, together with a massless spin-2 particle, force equality of gravitational and inertial mass / universal soft gravitational coupling. Weinberg (1965) then showed that consistent perturbative graviton interactions require Einstein-type field equations.

Source anchors:
- Steven Weinberg, `Photons and Gravitons in S-Matrix Theory: Derivation of Charge Conservation and Equality of Gravitational and Inertial Mass`, Phys. Rev. 135, B1049 (1964), DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.135.B1049.
- Steven Weinberg, `Photons and Gravitons in Perturbation Theory: Derivation of Maxwell's and Einstein's Equations`, Phys. Rev. 138, B988 (1965), DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.138.B988.

### Route B — Local horizon thermodynamics
Jacobson (1995) derived the Einstein equation by requiring `delta Q = T dS` for all local Rindler causal horizons, with entropy proportional to horizon area and heat identified with energy flux through the horizon.

Source anchor:
- Ted Jacobson, `Thermodynamics of Spacetime: The Einstein Equation of State`, Phys. Rev. Lett. 75, 1260 (1995), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.75.1260.

### Route C — Spacetime-deformation consistency
Hojman, Kuchar, and Teitelboim (1976) recovered canonical Einstein geometrodynamics by demanding a Hamiltonian representation consistent with the deformation algebra of spacelike hypersurfaces in spacetime. The route shows that consistent composition of local deformations is a severe structural constraint on gravitational dynamics.

Source anchor:
- S. A. Hojman, K. Kuchar, C. Teitelboim, `Geometrodynamics regained`, Annals of Physics 96, 88-135 (1976), DOI 10.1016/0003-4916(76)90112-3.

## Structures that survive the comparison

### 1. Relational / causal structure
All successful routes require a rule for which physical events, states, or local deformations can influence or follow one another.

Candidate role:
`C = causal / relational reachability structure`.

### 2. Measure / scale structure
Causal order alone does not provide the full physical metric scale. Gravity must also encode quantities such as proper intervals, area, volume, energy scale, and the normalization set by Newton's constant / equivalent coupling data.

Candidate role:
`M = measure / scale structure`.

### 3. Physical state and flow
The gravitational response depends on matter-energy / momentum flow or an equivalent source representation. Jacobson's horizon derivation uses energy flux; Einstein gravity uses stress-energy; spin-2 consistency enforces universal coupling.

Candidate role:
`Phi = physical state and conserved / transported flows`.

### 4. Universal response and backreaction
Gravity cannot be treated as a one-way external influence. The gravitational field / geometry participates in the dynamics and self-interacts. The response changes subsequent propagation, causal accessibility, and future state evolution.

Candidate role:
`R = universal self-consistent response / backreaction rule`.

### 5. Consistent composition of local transformations
HKT adds an especially important requirement: local evolution/deformation operations cannot compose arbitrarily. Their algebra must close consistently if they are to represent one coherent spacetime evolution.

Candidate role:
`A = composition / closure algebra for allowed gravitational transformations`.
