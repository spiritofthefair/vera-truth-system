# 1. Hawking failure boundary

Hawking's 1976 formulation proposed that black-hole formation and evaporation can map an initially pure state to a mixed final density matrix for the accessible exterior, replacing an ordinary S matrix with a superscattering map.

Schematic historical form:

`rho_in = |psi><psi|`

`rho_out = $[rho_in]`

with `rho_out` mixed in the information-losing interpretation.

The central conflict is not that density matrices are forbidden. Reduced density matrices are normal in quantum mechanics.

The conflict is:

`GLOBAL PURE INITIAL STATE`
`-> COMPLETE EVAPORATION / NO ACCOUNTED COMPLEMENT`
`-> FUNDAMENTALLY MIXED TOTAL FINAL STATE`

if ordinary closed-system unitary quantum evolution is assumed.

Therefore the first closure rule remains:

`LOCAL / ACCESSIBLE INFORMATION LOSS != GLOBAL INFORMATION DESTRUCTION`.

# 2. Page constraint

For a globally pure bipartite quantum system, a subsystem may have large entropy because it is entangled with its complement.

Page's 1993 subsystem-entropy result quantifies the typical entropy behavior of a subsystem of a larger pure state, and his black-hole information analysis emphasized that if black-hole formation/evaporation is described by an S matrix, information should eventually be present in the radiation, though potentially in highly distributed correlations.

The operational implication is:

If black-hole evaporation is globally unitary and the black hole disappears completely, the fine-grained entropy of the radiation cannot increase monotonically forever. It must eventually decrease toward the entropy appropriate to the final global state.

This is the Page-curve constraint.

It is a conditional consequence of global unitarity and the assumed system accounting; it is not direct observational proof that astrophysical black holes follow a Page curve.

# 3. The key distinction: dynamics versus accessibility

The Theory-of-Everything bridge is

`T = (D, B, M_obs)`.

At a horizon, `M_obs` cannot be treated as a passive fixed readout map.

Gravity changes:
- causal accessibility;
- which observables an observer can operationally access;
- which degrees of freedom are traced over in an effective description;
- the physical meaning of a subsystem boundary;
- the relation between local and global descriptions.

Therefore define a dynamical accessibility/measurement map:

`M_obs(t) = F[gravitational state, observer, causal accessibility, boundary conditions, resolution, channel]`.

Then even if the complete state follows one global law,

`rho_global(t) = D_*[rho_global(0)]`,

the observer may infer

`rho_accessible(t) = Restrict_{M_obs(t)}[rho_global(t)]`.

The accessible state can become mixed without requiring the global state to become mixed.

This does not solve the paradox by itself because complete evaporation demands an accounting of what constitutes the final total system. It does, however, prevent a category error between inaccessible information and destroyed information.
